# ORDINAL FIELD

*A design philosophy for the printed adjudication.*

---

## The movement

**Ordinal Field** is the aesthetic of the comparative plate: many specimens, one light,
one ruled ground, and rank made visible as a property of the field rather than as a
verdict announced at the top. It descends from the botanical monograph and the
type-specimen sheet — surfaces on which rival things are laid down side by side under
identical conditions so that the *difference* becomes the subject, and the observer's
opinion is deliberately the last thing the page carries. It is a philosophy for
documents that must be trusted rather than enjoyed, and its ambition is that every
mark on the page should look as though it was placed by someone who had already
removed four others.

## Space and form

The field is a single wide measure of warm paper interrupted by hairlines, never by
boxes. Rules are the only structural furniture: one weight for the field edge, one
hairline for the internal division, and nothing heavier anywhere. Specimens — the
captured frames, the tabulated ranks — sit *within* the measure with an inset margin
that reads as mounting, not cropping. Air is not decoration here; it is the control
condition, and a page that runs out of air has stopped being an observation and
become an argument. Every column edge, every baseline, every inset must align to a
single spatial scale, and where an element cannot align it is removed. This alignment
discipline is the labour of the piece: painstaking, invisible when it succeeds, and
instantly legible as amateurism the moment one rule falls a half-point off its
neighbour.

## Colour and material

The palette is unprinted stock, iron-gall ink, and one oxide accent, and it holds at three
values because a fourth would begin to encode meaning the data does not carry. Ink is
near-black and warm, never a screen's pure black — the ground is paper and the ink
must sit *on* it, not punch a hole through it. The oxide is reserved: it marks a first
place, a rupture, a withheld figure, and nothing else, and its scarcity is the whole
of its force. Grey is not a weakened ink; it is a separate substance for apparatus —
provenance, folio, the source line — and it must remain fully legible at its smallest
size, because a citation set below the threshold of sight is a citation that was never
made. The material logic is a plate printed once, carefully, on good stock: no
gradients, no shadows, no glass, no glow. Anything that suggests a screen breaks the
frame.

## Scale and rhythm

Scale is spent exactly twice per section: once on the section's number, which is
enormous and quiet, and once on the claim, which is large, set in the display serif,
tightly led and generously spaced beneath. Everything else falls into a narrow,
disciplined band of sizes, and the monospace face carries all apparatus — labels,
ranks, measurements, file paths — so the eye learns within one page that monospace
means *this is traceable*. Rhythm comes from repetition rather than variety: the same
plate, the same caption geometry, the same rule above every table, repeated until the
repetition itself becomes the argument that the comparison was fair. Repetition of
this kind is the most expensive thing on the page to execute well, and it must show
the hours — the fifth plate exactly as placed as the first, or the whole claim of
even-handedness collapses.

## Composition and balance

Each section opens on a numbered field and closes on a rule; nothing floats. Tabulated
material is set as a field of figures, not a grid of cells — no fills, no zebra
striping, no borders around numbers, because a border tells the reader the number
needed help. Images are specimens: bounded by a hairline, captioned in monospace
beneath with the tool, the mode and the file the frame came from, and never allowed to
bleed. Where two things are compared they sit at identical size on a shared baseline,
and where they cannot — because one is a still and one is a frame lifted from a film —
the caption says so rather than the layout pretending otherwise. Balance is achieved
by weight, not by centring: the page is asymmetrical, anchored left, and its outer
margin carries the apparatus that lets a reader check the work. Master-level execution
here means nothing overlaps, nothing falls off the plate, and every element has
breathing room that was measured rather than guessed.

## Visual hierarchy

The order of reading is fixed and enforced by size alone: section number, claim,
evidence, apparatus. A reader who reads only the claims must come away with the whole
argument; a reader who reads only the apparatus must be able to reproduce every
figure. Nothing in between may compete. Type is set for paper — a warm text serif at a
generous measure, a display serif used sparingly and at real size, a neutral monospace
for everything checkable. Body copy never drops below the point at which a printed
page is comfortable, and apparatus never drops below the point at which it can be read
in ordinary light: the exact discipline the field being reported on failed, in five of
six tools, and the one defect this document is least entitled to repeat. The result
must read as the product of deep expertise and countless hours — a page that appears
to have been *settled* rather than assembled, in which a reader hunting for a mistake
finds only decisions.

## The subtle reference

The quiet conceptual thread is **the assay plate**: a comparative sheet on which
samples of unknown quality are tested against one another under identical conditions,
and on which the honest result is sometimes that the samples cannot be separated. That
is exactly the finding this document carries — one mode produced a clean rank order,
the other produced dispersion — so the plate is the right form for it. The reference
is never named on the page. Someone who knows what a specimen sheet is will feel it in
the hairline insets and the repeated caption geometry; everyone else simply reads a
carefully set report.

## Expression constraints for this instance

- **Unbranded.** No house marks, no house palette, no house type. The document names
  the bake-off, never its sponsor.
- **Print first.** A4, one column, real margins, ink on paper. Screen is a preview of
  the print, never the target.
- **Fonts.** Display: Young Serif. Text: Lora (regular / italic / bold). Apparatus:
  Geist Mono (regular / bold). All embedded as base64, all open-licensed (OFL), all
  from the canvas-design bundle.
- **Palette.** The ground is the stock itself — unprinted paper, `#FFFFFF` on
  screen — so the warmth lives entirely in the ink rather than in a printed tint.
  Ink `#1B1A17`, warm and never a screen's pure black. Apparatus grey `#5F5B51`.
  Hairline `#D2CCBF`. Oxide accent `#8A3324`, reserved for rank-1 marks, the single
  rupture, and withheld figures. Deliberately *not* the house dark surface and *not*
  the house accent.
- **Every figure carries its source in the reader's line of sight**, in monospace,
  because the house content gate requires it and because this movement holds that a
  measurement without its apparatus is only an assertion.
