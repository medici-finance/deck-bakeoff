The problem

RWA lending's hardest problem is the liquidation nobody can execute

  • Every venue prices your collateral against a liquidation threshold.
  • Tokenized funds cannot be sold in one transaction.
  • So the workout is hand-built: signed liquidation agreements with issuers, formal de-leverage frameworks, curators with a line to the issuer.

RedStone, March 2026: these are “not edge cases but fundamental design constraints”.

When the threshold does break — Black Thursday

$8M of collateral liquidated for $0.

The solution

One position becomes two claims that always sum back to it

  • P, the Protected leg — full collateral value at or below strike, no margin call, no forced sale.
  • N, the Amplified leg — everything above strike.
  • P + N is identically the collateral, at every price, by construction.
  • Split, hold, then recombine or settle at maturity — atomically.
Medici Canton briefing slides 3 · 5; Medici asset-manager briefing slide 3; Medici brand guide §8.2 03

The mechanism

At maturity, P takes S/x and N takes the rest

x ≤ S P receives 1.00 N receives 0.00
x > S P receives S/x N receives 1 − S/x
  • Every row sums to exactly the collateral — that flat top edge is the invariant.
  • Above strike, P declines continuously — a slope, never a cliff.
  • The oracle is read once, at settlement, not continuously.

The proof

No liquidation threshold exists because no under-collateralization condition exists

  • “No forced liquidation” is a theorem, not a risk parameter.
  • Closed-form invariant, verified as pure math — not a runtime assert.
  • There is no price x at which the vault is short of collateral.
  • Nothing to breach, so nothing to seize, so no liquidation agreement to sign.
Medici Canton briefing slide 5; Medici asset-manager briefing slide 4 05

Two holders

Two legs, two holders, each with an intrinsic reason

Fund treasury or issuer takes P

Income on an asset they keep — never sold, never called. Selling P is the liquidity, with no repayment schedule.

Allocator or speculator takes N

Amplified exposure, no liquidation price, no funding rate.

Endogenous two-sided demand

Volatile collateral plus two intrinsic holders equals demand on both sides of the split.

The volatility caveat

The split needs volatility to price N: treasuries have least of it, gold and equity-linked most.
